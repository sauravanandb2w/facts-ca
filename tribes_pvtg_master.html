What are PVTGs?

Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups — a subset of Scheduled Tribes on a separate MoTA list (not every ST is a PVTG). Earlier called Primitive Tribal Groups (PTGs); renamed PVTG by the Government (MoTA).

Why a special category? ST communities that need higher-priority protection and development — typically very small populations, geographic isolation, subsistence livelihoods, low literacy, and high vulnerability to displacement (forests, dams, conservation projects).

Identification (prelims): MoTA uses vulnerability indicators such as — pre-agricultural level of technology; stagnant or declining population; extremely low literacy; subsistence-level economy. List is reviewed and updated (e.g. 75 groups on the 2023 list).

Timeline: Dhebar Commission (1960–61) stressed special focus on the most “primitive” tribal groups → 1975 central PVTG development programme (started with 52 groups) → expanded to 75. Odisha has the highest number of PVTGs among states. Policy links on Law tab: FRA habitat rights, PM-JANMAN (2023).

      Exam facts capsule (poster + Census / Constitution)

      • ST population: 8.6% of India (2011 Census).
      • PVTGs: 75 groups (2023 list); Odisha has the highest number of PVTGs among states.
      • Bhil — largest ST community by population (often cited ~38% of ST population in infographic sources — verify with Census tables).
      • Madhya Pradesh — highest ST population among states (2011).
      • Santhal — Manjhi-Paragana traditional governance (MoTA); clusters of 50–200 villages.
      • Lakshadweep — indigenous population notified as ST.
      • Art. 342 — President specifies ST list; Art. 275(1) — grants for Scheduled Areas / ST welfare.

      MoTA PDF — 16 tribes (expand each)

      From MoTA — Tribal Faces in India + Census / TRIFED context. Same card shape as : Who & where → Livelihood → Culture → Exam lens → Today. Yellow = prelims term. Summary table on .

      Bhil — CG, GJ, KA, MP, MH, AP, RJ
      Who & where
      Among India’s largest ST communities; name from billu (bow); spread across western & central India (MP, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Andhra).
      Livelihood
      Agriculture, forest produce, seasonal labour migration; traditional hunting & archery heritage; healers Budwa / Huvarki / Vaidu.
      Culture & festivals
      Toranmal fair; forest deities; Bhilala subgroup; links to Ghoomar/Garba regions.
      Exam lens
      Often cited as largest ST group by population (verify Census 2011 tables); Bhil revolts (1818–28); Fifth Schedule blocks in MP/RJ.
      Today
      Land alienation, Narmada/Aravalli project peripheries; ST status; not PVTG.
      Gond — MP, CG, MH, AP, TS, Odisha…
      Who & where
      Major Dravidian-language Gondi belt — “Gondwana” heartland (Satpura, Bastar, Vidarbha); sub-groups Raj / Madia / Dhurve / Khatulwar.
      Livelihood
      Millets kodo & kutki; agriculture; tendu leaves; historic bewar shifting cultivation in forest tracts.
      Culture & festivals
      Clan totems; Bastar Dussehra (tribal festival distinct from plains Dussehra); see Madia Gond for Ghotul.
      Exam lens
      Kanha, Pench, Achanakmar landscapes; Gond kingdom history (Garha-Mandla); overlap with Maria groups in Bastar.
      Today
      Major ST; mining & conservation vs forest rights (FRA); some pockets linked to PVTG policy debates.
      Santhal — AS, BR, CG, JH, OD, WB
      Who & where
      Large Austro-Asiatic community on Chotanagpur plateau & Rajmahal hills; Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Bihar, Assam.
      Livelihood
      Rice agriculture, forest produce, sericulture; traditional hunting; Manjhi-Paragana governs clusters of 50–200 villages (MoTA).
      Culture & festivals
      Spirits Marang Buru / Bonga; sacred groves; Sohrai, Karam; puppetry Chadar Badar.
      Exam lens
      Santhal rebellion (1855) — Sidhu-Kanhu; Fifth Schedule Jharkhand; Dalma sanctuary region.
      Today
      ST (not PVTG); displacement from coal/iron ore belts; literacy rising but livelihood stress persists.
      Munda — Chotanagpur (JH, CG, MP, OD, TR, WB)
      Who & where
      Core tribe of Chotanagpur mosaic; surnames from nature; memorial stone sasandiri; Munda languages (Austro-Asiatic).
      Livelihood
      Plateau agriculture; forest labour; historically linked to tea estates & mines in Jharkhand belt.
      Culture & festivals
      Magha & Ba festivals; dance Nupur; clan organisation; overlaps with Manki-Munda vocabulary in governance MCQs.
      Exam lens
      Munda rebellion (1899–1900) / Birsa Munda movement context in same region; Jharkhand statehood narrative.
      Today
      ST; urban migration to Ranchi/Jamshedpur; land conflicts with industry.
      Baiga PVTG — CG, JH, BR, OD, WB, MP, UP
      Who & where
      Forest tribe of central India; known as “priests of the forest”; Mandla–Balaghat–Bilaspur belt; PVTG.
      Livelihood
      Bewar (slash-and-burn) historically; forest produce; restrictions inside protected forests push wage labour.
      Culture & festivals
      Life-stage tattoos; abandon & rebuild house after death; forest-spirit rites more than named state fairs (MoTA).
      Exam lens
      Textbook PVTG; Kanha NP periphery displacement; PM-JANMAN / habitat rights under FRA.
      Today
      Conservation vs livelihood; sedentarisation schemes; health & literacy gaps.
      Birhor PVTG — Jharkhand & neighbours
      Who & where
      Tiny nomadic forest community; camps tanda; Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh forests; PVTG.
      Livelihood
      Honey, monkey-hair rope nets, hunting; barter with settled villages; extreme dependence on intact forest.
      Culture & festivals
      No large settled festival calendar — mobility & band size define social life.
      Exam lens
      Smallest & most vulnerable PVTG examples in prelims; Palamau / Fifth Schedule context.
      Today
      Sedentarisation, forest closure, very low population — high policy priority under PVTG schemes.
      Korku — MP, Maharashtra (Melghat), CG
      Who & where
      Central Indian forest tribe; Melghat (Amravati) & Satpura foothills; Korku language — UNESCO endangered.
      Livelihood
      Agriculture (early potato/coffee adoption in tribal belts); forest labour; Melghat tiger reserve interface.
      Culture & festivals
      Flute music at Diwali; Munda memorial pillar for deceased.
      Exam lens
      Language endangerment + conservation (Melghat); pair with Korku–Gond–Nihal region map memory.
      Today
      Malnutrition & health campaigns in Melghat; ST; not PVTG.
      Madia Gond — CG, MH, MP, OD
      Who & where
      Isolated Gond branch — Bada / Chota Madia; Abujhmarh forest (Chhattisgarh–Maharashtra).
      Livelihood
      Shifting cultivation, forest food; limited market access until recent road projects.
      Culture & festivals
      Ghotul youth dormitory (education & courtship institution); groom carves Mundha marriage pillar.
      Exam lens
      Ghotul is a favourite MCQ trap — link to Madia/Muria Gond, not all Gonds.
      Today
      Security & development debates in Abujhmarh; ST; cultural institutions under modern schooling pressure.
      Abhuj Maria — Bastar, Chhattisgarh
      Who & where
      “Abhuj” = unknown hills; isolated Maria Gond subgroup in Bastar; distinctive iron neck rings (women).
      Livelihood
      Shifting cultivation, forest gathering; low external contact historically.
      Culture & festivals
      Kaksar (June) & Kudin festivals; Maria dance traditions within Bastar cultural complex.
      Exam lens
      Bastar Dussehra ecosystem; do not confuse with Bison Horn Maria dress code.
      Today
      ST; Bastar insurgency & welfare scheme delivery challenges.
      Bison Horn Maria — Bastar, CG, MH, MP
      Who & where
      Maria Gond group named for wedding bison-horn headdress; Bastar & border districts.
      Livelihood
      Shifting cultivation; agriculture on forest clearings; clan deity at village edge.
      Culture & festivals
      Marriage dance with horn headdress (MoTA portrait motif); agricultural cycle rituals.
      Exam lens
      Visual culture MCQs; Bastar ethnography; distinct from Abhuj Maria jewellery.
      Today
      ST; tourism exposure via Bastar fairs; land pressure.
      Warli — MH, GJ, KA, Goa, DNH & DD
      Who & where
      Western Ghats & coastal hinterland (Maharashtra–Gujarat border); Warli (Varli) language; nature-worshipping community.
      Livelihood
      Rice, pulses, forest produce; wage labour on plantations & Mumbai periphery.
      Culture & festivals
      Warli painting encodes cosmology (not decorative-only); mahua / toddy in social rites; harvest ceremonies.
      Exam lens
      GS-I art & culture; GI / tribal art commercialisation; Dahanu–Palghar belt.
      Today
      ST; urbanisation & industrial corridors vs tribal hamlets.
      Kokna — GJ, MH, KA, RJ, DNH
      Who & where
      Konkan-linked tribal community; Gujarat & Maharashtra ghats; traditional village panchayat.
      Livelihood
      Mixed farming & forest produce; labour migration to cities.
      Culture & festivals
      Bohada mask festival — high-yield prelims name; folk theatre traditions.
      Exam lens
      Konkan tribal belt map; distinguish Kokna vs Koli (fishing) vs Warli (art).
      Today
      ST; highway & dam projects in Western Ghats.
      Ka Thakar — Maharashtra (Thane, Raigad…)
      Who & where
      Forest hamlets Thakarwadi; Mumbai metropolitan forest fringe (Thane, Raigad, Nashik).
      Livelihood
      Bamboo craft, forest produce; seasonal migration.
      Culture & festivals
      Chibra oil-lamp ritual at Diwali; oral folklore.
      Exam lens
      Urban sprawl vs tribal hamlets; Maharashtra ST list nuance.
      Today
      ST; encroachment & forest rights claims under FRA.
      Koli Dhor — Western Ghats, Mumbai region
      Who & where
      Pastoral Koli subgroup — dhor = cattle; Western Ghats & Mumbai region (distinct from coastal Koli fishermen).
      Livelihood
      Cattle, hide work historically; now dominant pattern is informal urban wage labour.
      Culture & festivals
      Village fairs; pastoral identity symbols; overlap with Mahadev Koli narratives in MH.
      Exam lens
      Livelihood transition MCQs; don’t merge all “Koli” subgroups.
      Today
      ST in Maharashtra; slum rehabilitation & loss of grazing corridors.
      Pahari Korwa — CG (Jashpur, Sarguja)
      Who & where
      Hill Korwa in north Chhattisgarh; Mundari language branch; Jashpur–Surguja forests.
      Livelihood
      Forest medicine knowledge; agriculture & MFP collection.
      Culture & festivals
      Dance with dhank / mandar drums; folk songs.
      Exam lens
      Name trap: Pahari Korwa ≠ Paharia PVTG (Jharkhand–Bengal) — separate groups.
      Today
      ST; mining & forest diversion in north CG.
      Kamar — Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh
      Who & where
      Small ST community in CG–MP foothills; bamboo-craft specialists.
      Livelihood
      Bamboo baskets & tools; agriculture; forest wage work.
      Culture & festivals
      Family deity symbol — iron chain + peacock feathers (MoTA portrait motif).
      Exam lens
      Pair geographically with Baiga / Korwa; central Indian craft tribes.
      Today
      ST; market linkages via haats & TRIFED-style MFP trade.

      More Indian ST profiles (beyond MoTA 16)

      Same five-field cards. Bhil, Gond, Santhal, Baiga, Birhor, etc. are in the MoTA block above — not repeated here. Sources: MoTA, Census India, state tribal research institutes. Terms: Jhum, Podu/Bewar, Ghotul, Transhumance.

      Toda — Nilgiris (Tamil Nadu)
      Who & where
      Pastoral ST of Nilgiri plateau; live alongside Kota, Irula, Kurumba, Badaga in “ethnic mosaic”.
      Livelihood
      Buffalo pastoralism — milk products; sacred dairy temples; limited agriculture.
      Culture & festivals
      Barrel-vaulted huts; buffalo rituals; patrilineal clans; seasonal pastoral movements on plateau.
      Exam lens
      Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve; Mudumalai NP; compare matrilineal Khasi vs patrilineal Toda.
      Today
      Small population ST; tea-estate encroachment on grazing lands.
      Naga — Nagaland, Manipur, Arunachal, Assam
      Who & where
      Many sub-tribes (Angami, Ao, Sema, Lotha, etc.) in Patkai–Naga hills; Tibeto-Burman languages.
      Livelihood
      Jhum on hill slopes; horticulture, handicrafts, growing urban service sector.
      Culture & festivals
      Hornbill Festival (Nagaland showcase); village councils; Christian majority with indigenous festivals retained.
      Exam lens
      Article 371A (Nagaland); Inner Line Permit; insurgency & peace accords history.
      Today
      ST; border trade & connectivity projects; youth migration to cities.
      Khasi & Garo — Meghalaya
      Who & where
      Khasi, Jaintia & Garo hills; among India’s well-known matrilineal ST societies (youngest daughter often inherits).
      Livelihood
      Betel nut, areca, horticulture; coal & limestone economy debates in Garo hills.
      Culture & festivals
      Nongkrem (Khasi), Wangala (Garo); monoliths; living root bridges (Khasi-Jaintia craft).
      Exam lens
      Sixth Schedule ADCs in Meghalaya; Nokrek BR; Balpakram NP (Garo).
      Today
      Relatively higher literacy; mining ban & livelihood transitions.
      Chenchu PVTG — Andhra Pradesh / Telangana
      Who & where
      Forest-dwelling ST of Nallamala hills; Chenchu language (Dravidian); PVTG.
      Livelihood
      Honey collection, hunting, forest foods; increasing wage labour & resettlement.
      Culture & festivals
      Forest deity cults; oral epics; seasonal forest camps.
      Exam lens
      Amrabad Tiger Reserve; Nagarjunsagar–Srisailam; tiger reserve vs tribal rights.
      Today
      Health & literacy gaps; eco-tourism & road projects into forests.
      Bonda PVTG — Odisha (Malkangiri)
      Who & where
      Isolated hill community in Bonda hills; Malkangiri district; Odisha has highest PVTG count among states.
      Livelihood
      Podu shifting cultivation; forest produce; weekly barter at Onukudelli market (exam landmark).
      Culture & festivals
      Women’s distinctive ringa skirt; Bonda–Gadaba cultural cluster; strong identity markers.
      Exam lens
      Mainland isolation PVTG; Eastern Ghats map; Niyamgiri/Dongria separate but often grouped in OD tribal MCQs.
      Today
      Malnutrition & education programmes; road connectivity changing isolation.
      Katkari PVTG — Maharashtra
      Who & where
      Western Ghats foothills (Raigad, Thane, Pune belt); Katkari language; PVTG.
      Livelihood
      Forest labour, charcoal historically; bonded-labour legacy documented in welfare reports.
      Culture & festivals
      Forest spirits; small hamlets; oral traditions.
      Exam lens
      Sahyadri tiger landscape; PVTG + labour exploitation themes in society optional.
      Today
      Housing & education schemes; ST; urban fringe encroachment.
      Gujjar & Bakarwal — J&K, Ladakh, HP, UK
      Who & where
      Pastoral ST/scheduled communities in Himalayas; Bakarwal often listed as ST in J&K/Ladakh context; Gujjar spread across north-west.
      Livelihood
      Sheep/goat & cattle; seasonal transhumance to alpine meadows (e.g. Kashmir–Rajouri routes).
      Culture & festivals
      Mobile tents; kinship clans; syncretic Islam with pastoral customs.
      Exam lens
      Transhumance definition; conflict over forest grazing rights; Van Gujjar (UP) related but distinct.
      Today
      Climate change on pasture timing; sedentarisation & highway projects.
      Great Andamanese · Onge · Jarawa · Sentinelese · Shompen
      Who & where
      Andaman (Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, Sentinelese) & Great Nicobar (Shompen); among most isolated peoples in India.
      Livelihood
      Foraging, fishing, horticulture (varies); Jarawa/Sentinelese historically low contact.
      Culture & festivals
      Distinct languages; Jarawa voluntary isolation broken by Andaman Trunk Road debates; Sentinelese reject contact.
      Exam lens
      All PVTG; Restricted Area Permit; Supreme Court orders on ATR & Jarawa welfare; Sentinel legal protection.
      Today
      Great Andamanese few survivors; disease risk from outsiders; Shompen post-2004 tsunami rehabilitation on Great Nicobar.
      Lakshadweep — indigenous ST population
      Who & where
      Only UT where entire indigenous population notified as ST (Malayali Mahls / islanders); coral atolls in Laccadive Sea.
      Livelihood
      Tuna fishing, coconut economy; maritime skills; limited land area.
      Culture & festivals
      Islamic calendar festivals; boat-building; island-specific dialects.
      Exam lens
      Unique ST geography MCQ; coral ecology; compare with Andaman hunter-gatherer STs.
      Today
      Climate & sea-level risk; infrastructure & tourism debates.

      DNT note

      DNT Denotified Tribes — colonial Criminal Tribes Act 1871 (repealed 1952). Not same as ST list; National Commission for DNTs, SNTs & NTs (Renke/Idate commissions). Many DNT communities overlap socially with nomadic tribes but may not be ST.

        Maasai — Kenya & Tanzania
        Who & where
        Semi-nomadic Nilotic pastoralists on East African savanna (Kenya & northern Tanzania); Maa language; livelihood tied to cattle, sheep, and goats.
        Livelihood & food
        Milk daily staple; dry-season blood mixed with milk from cattle; meat mainly at ceremonies and markets. Traditionally little agriculture — land seen as pasture, not farms.
        Culture
        Cattle = wealth; age-sets and moran (warrior) phase; beadwork; rites such as Eunoto; seasonal transhumance.
        Exam lens
        Pastoralism vs conservation (Mara/Serengeti); nutrition debates need context (activity, genetics, diet change since 1970s) — not a plug for modern “carnivore” diets.
        Today
        Tourism, fenced reserves, climate stress on pasture; rising NCDs as diets modernise.
        San (Basarwa) — Botswana, Namibia, South Africa
        Who & where
        Indigenous hunter-gatherers of the Kalahari; Khoisan language family with click consonants; among the oldest continuous cultures in southern Africa.
        Livelihood & food
        Hunting (e.g. small game, historically poison arrows), gathering tubers and nuts (e.g. mongongo); water from roots in dry seasons; contrast with neighbouring pastoral Bantu groups.
        Culture
        Small band organisation; trance healing dances; rich rock art (several UNESCO World Heritage sites in the region).
        Exam lens
        Persistence hunting case studies; indigenous rights vs protected areas — Central Kalahari Game Reserve (Botswana) evictions and court rulings.
        Today
        Marginalisation, wage labour on farms; language and land-loss pressures.
        Tuareg — Mali, Niger, Algeria, Libya
        Who & where
        Berber-speaking (Tamacheq) pastoralists of the Sahara & Sahel; historically trans-Saharan trade links; often called “Blue People” for indigo-dyed tagelmust veils.
        Livelihood & food
        Camel and goat herding; caravan trade (salt, dates, goods); millet and dates when settled; oasis agriculture in pockets.
        Culture
        Clan confederations; oral poetry and music (tinde drum); matrilineal elements in some lineages; silver jewellery craft.
        Exam lens
        Sahel insecurity, drought, and state borders vs nomadic routes; uranium mining (e.g. Niger) and pastoral displacement.
        Today
        Rebellions and peace accords; sedentarisation; climate-driven pasture stress.
        Himba — Namibia (Kunene)
        Who & where
        Semi-nomadic pastoralists in arid Kunene region (north-west Namibia); related to Herero; speak Otji-Himba dialect.
        Livelihood & food
        Cattle and goats; milk and maize porridge; men often herd, women manage homesteads; scarce rainfall drives mobility.
        Culture
        Women’s otjize (ochre + butterfat) paste on skin and hair; sacred okuruwo (sacred fire) tended by women; strong kinship and ancestor veneration.
        Exam lens
        Indigenous tourism ethics; drought and land tenure in arid Africa; contrast with San hunter-gatherers in same country.
        Today
        Modernisation, schooling, and conservation areas affecting grazing.
        Forest peoples (“Pygmy” groups) — Central Africa
        Who & where
        Diverse forest-dwelling hunter-gatherer and horticultural peoples (e.g. Baka, Mbuti, Aka) in Congo Basin rainforests — Cameroon, CAR, DRC, Gabon, etc. (UNESCO & UN use community-specific names; “Pygmy” is an external label.)
        Livelihood & food
        Hunting, fishing, honey collection, and forest gathering; exchange with farming neighbours; deep ethnobotanical knowledge.
        Culture
        Egalitarian bands; polyphonic yodel-style singing; forest as spiritual and economic base.
        Exam lens
        Logging, mining, and national parks vs indigenous forest use; World Bank / conservation resettlement debates.
        Today
        Discrimination, loss of forest access, and push for land rights under national and UN frameworks.
        Inuit — Arctic Canada, Greenland, Alaska, Chukotka
        Who & where
        Indigenous peoples of the Arctic circumpolar north; Inuit (Canada/Greenland) and related Iñupiat/Yupik in Alaska; sea-ice and tundra homelands.
        Livelihood & food
        Marine mammal hunting (seal, walrus, whale where permitted), fish, and caribou; muktuk and other traditional foods; sharing economy (“country food”).
        Culture
        Kayak, umiaq, iglu/sod houses; sophisticated sea-ice knowledge; throat singing (where practised); modern political voice via Inuit Circumpolar Council.
        Exam lens
        Fastest warming region — ice loss breaks hunting safety and food security; sovereignty, shipping lanes, and resource extraction.
        Today
        Food security, housing, and self-government agreements; climate adaptation funding debates.
        Sami — Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia
        Who & where
        Only indigenous people recognised across Fennoscandia; traditional lands span taiga, tundra, and coast; Sami languages (several variants).
        Livelihood & food
        Reindeer herding (coastal fishing and farming in some areas); reindeer meat, fish, and berries; herding follows seasonal migration routes.
        Culture
        Joik (traditional song); duodji crafts; Siida (reindeer herding communities); Sámi Parliament (Norway) and similar bodies.
        Exam lens
        ILO 169 politics (ratified in Norway/Finland, not Sweden until debates); wind farms and mines vs grazing corridors.
        Today
        Land-use conflicts, language education, and EU/indigenous consultation norms.
        Bedouin — Arabian Peninsula & Sahara fringes
        Who & where
        Arabic-speaking nomadic and formerly nomadic tribes of deserts from Arabian Peninsula to North Africa; patrilineal clans (qabila).
        Livelihood & food
        Camel and goat pastoralism; dates and coffee hospitality; trade and raiding historically; today also wage labour and urban ties.
        Culture
        Black goat-hair tents; oral poetry; strict hospitality codes; tribal law (urf) alongside state law.
        Exam lens
        Sedentarisation after nation-states and oil economies; border controls ending open desert mobility.
        Today
        Citizenship gaps for some groups; cultural revival via heritage festivals and tourism.
        Ainu — Japan (Hokkaido)
        Who & where
        Indigenous people of Hokkaido (and historic presence in Sakhalin/Kurils); recognised in Japan’s 2019 law promoting Ainu culture.
        Livelihood & food
        Salmon and trout fishing; hunting (bear, deer); gathering wild plants; traditional milu (fermented fish) and acorn-based foods.
        Culture
        Iomante (bear ceremony, now rare); Ainu language revitalisation; oral epics (yukar); patterns on clothing (Ainu moshiri worldview).
        Exam lens
        Assimilation era (“former aborigines” policy lifted 1997); Upopoy National Ainu Museum; land and resource rights still limited vs Canada/NZ models.
        Today
        Tourism, cultural centres, and UNPFII engagement; discrimination and identity recognition ongoing.
        Kazakhs — Kazakhstan & Central Asia
        Who & where
        Turkic steppe pastoralists; majority population of Kazakhstan; also Xinjiang, Uzbekistan, etc.
        Livelihood & food
        Horse and sheep herding; kumis (fermented mare’s milk), meat, dairy; beshbarmak (national dish); winter fodder on steppe.
        Culture
        Yurt (kiiz uy); epic poetry (Korkyt); eagle hunting stronger among Kyrgyz neighbours but shared Altai heritage.
        Exam lens
        Soviet collectivisation (Virgin Lands) and famine memory; post-1991 nation-building and nomad heritage revival.
        Today
        Urban majority but state promotion of traditional games (kokpar, etc.) and UNESCO intangible heritage listings.
        Yanomami — Amazon (Brazil / Venezuela)
        Who & where
        Large Amazonian indigenous nation (~35k+); rainforest on Brazil–Venezuela border; Yanomami Territorial Reserve (Brazil) is one of the biggest indigenous territories.
        Livelihood & food
        Shifting cultivation (banana, manioc); hunting and fishing; communal gardens; protein from forest and rivers.
        Culture
        Round communal houses (shabono); shamans (xapiripë); ceremonial feasts and alliance exchanges; strong village autonomy.
        Exam lens
        Illegal gold mining (garimpo), mercury pollution, and disease from contact; FUNAI / health emergency declarations.
        Today
        NGO and government crackdowns on miners; debate over isolation vs integration.
        Quechua — Andes (Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador)
        Who & where
        Largest indigenous linguistic group in the Americas; descendants of Inca and pre-Inca Andean civilisations; high-altitude sierra and valleys.
        Livelihood & food
        Terraced agriculture; potato and quinoa diversity; llamas and alpacas for wool and transport; coca leaf in traditional use (distinct from illicit cocaine economy).
        Culture
        Quechua language (co-official in Peru/Bolivia); textiles with symbolic patterns; festivals (Inti Raymi heritage); ayllu community structure.
        Exam lens
        High-altitude adaptation; land reform and mining conflicts (e.g. copper, lithium); bilingual education policy.
        Today
        Political representation (e.g. Bolivia’s indigenous-led era); rural poverty and migration to cities.
        Maya — Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, Honduras
        Who & where
        Mesoamerican civilisation with millions of speakers today; lowland rainforest to highland Guatemala; UNESCO World Heritage sites (e.g. Tikal, Chichén Itzá).
        Livelihood & food
        Milpa (maize, beans, squash); cacao; turkey and forest game historically; modern mix of farming and labour.
        Culture
        Calendar and writing heritage (classic Maya script); woven huipil; 20+ Mayan languages; syncretic Catholic-indigenous festivals.
        Exam lens
        1996 Guatemala peace accords and indigenous rights; cultural continuity despite conquest; tourism vs sacred-site protection.
        Today
        Land rights for highland communities; language revitalisation in schools.
        Māori — Aotearoa (New Zealand)
        Who & where
        Indigenous Polynesian people of New Zealand; Te Tiriti o Waitangi (Treaty of Waitangi, 1840) is the founding document for Crown–Māori relations.
        Livelihood & food
        Historically horticulture (kumara), fishing, hunting; hāngī earth-oven cooking; today urban and rural economies plus seafood and agriculture.
        Culture
        Marae as social hub; whakapapa (genealogy); haka, carving, ta moko; Māori Language Week and co-official status efforts.
        Exam lens
        Waitangi Tribunal claims and settlements; co-governance (water, conservation); compare with Australian and Canadian treaty models.
        Today
        Demographic revival; debates on constitutional recognition and resource shares.
        Aboriginal Australians — many nations
        Who & where
        Hundreds of distinct nations (>250 language groups at contact); continent from desert to tropics; AIATSIS and NIAA document cultures (official Australian recognition).
        Livelihood & food
        Bush foods (macadamia, kangaroo, fish); seasonal harvesting; fire-stick farming to manage landscapes; waterholes and songline-linked country.
        Culture
        Dreaming / Dreamtime (songlines); art (dot painting traditions in Central Desert); oldest dated rock art; kinship systems.
        Exam lens
        Stolen Generations; Uluru Statement from the Heart (Voice referendum 2023); native title (Mabo 1992) vs mining.
        Today
        Closing the Gap targets; land and sea country management partnerships with parks.

        UN lens: UNDRIP 2007 (non-binding); ILO Convention 169 (binding where ratified — India not ratified). Useful for IR / society essays.
