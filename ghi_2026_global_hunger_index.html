Headline story (2026)

  • Global reversal: World GHI 18.1 (moderate) — higher than 17.6 in 2018, breaking the long decline from 28.2 (2000).
  • Theme: Hunger and insecurity are mutually reinforcing — food security must be in peace & security policy.
  • Drivers: Conflict (record state-based conflicts post-WWII), climate shocks, economic/trade disruption (Ukraine war, fertilizer prices, shipping risks).
  • Severity: 8 countries alarming; 36 serious; 32 countries worse than 2018.
  • SDG trajectory: At current pace since 2000, 57 countries won’t reach “low hunger” by 2030.
  • Hope: Nine countries cut GHI >20% since 2018 (e.g. Nepal, Rwanda, Togo, Uganda, Venezuela); long-run gains in Nepal, Sierra Leone, Tajikistan, Uganda.

How GHI is calculated

Composite index 0–100 (0 = no hunger). Four indicators, equally weighted, each rescaled per appendix formula:

  1. Undernourishment — % population with insufficient calorie intake (FAO).
  2. Child stunting — % under-5 with low height-for-age (UNICEF/WHO/World Bank).
  3. Child wasting — % under-5 with low weight-for-height (acute).
  4. Child mortality — % children dying before age five (UN IGME).

2026 data windows: undernourishment 2023–2025; wasting latest available; stunting 2024–2025; mortality 2024–2025 (see synopsis methodology).

Publishers: Welthungerhilfe (Germany) & Concern Worldwide (Ireland) — annual since 2006; not a UN index.

Three of four indicators worsened globally since 2018

Undernourishment, stunting, and wasting rose at global level; child mortality fell slightly — net global score up.
