From war to Charter — the full birth story

लीग फेल → डम्बारटन ओक्स खाका → सैन फ्रांसिस्को में संविधान → 24 अक्टूबर 1945 से UN “जीवित”
Sources: UN history, San Francisco Conference, Charter full text.

Timeline — how the UN was made

Aug 1941 Atlantic Charter (US & UK) — ideas of collective security & cooperation after WWII.
1 Jan 1942 Declaration by United Nations — 26 states at war with Axis pledge to fight together; name “United Nations” enters diplomacy.
1943 Moscow Declaration & Tehran — Allies commit to a general international organization.
Aug–Oct 1944 Dumbarton Oaks (Washington) — US, UK, USSR, China draft Proposals for a General International Organization (working text for San Francisco).
Feb 1945 Yalta — agreement on calling the San Francisco conference & formula for Security Council voting (P5 + non-permanent members).
25 Apr – 26 Jun 1945 UN Conference on International Organization, San Francisco — 50 delegations; adopt & sign UN Charter + ICJ Statute (26 June).
15 Oct 1945 Poland signs (no govt at conference date) → 51 founding members.
24 Oct 1945 Charter enters into force after ratification by P5 + majority of signatories → UN Day.

Who wrote the Charter?

  • Four sponsors at San Francisco: China, Soviet Union, United Kingdom, United States.
  • Delegates worked from Dumbarton Oaks proposals, Yalta understandings, and hundreds of amendments proposed by governments.
  • Charter has a Preamble + 111 Articles in 19 Chapters + ICJ Statute annexed.
  • India participated as a founding member (Indian delegation under British India at the conference — high-yield static + polity link).

Charter — purposes, principles, membership

ArticleWhat it says (exam core)
Art. 1Purposes: peace & security; friendly relations; cooperation; harmonize state action.
Art. 2Principles: sovereign equality; good faith; peaceful settlement; no threat of force; assist UN; non-intervention in domestic jurisdiction (SC enforcement exception).
Art. 3Original members = states at San Francisco + signatories of 1942 Declaration (as specified).
Art. 4New members: peace-loving states accepting obligations → SC recommendation + GA decision (see How it runs tab).
Art. 5–6Suspension / expulsion of members (rare; SC central).
Art. 108–109Charter amendment: 2/3 GA + ratification including all P5.
Art. 111Official languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish.

League of Nations → UN (why the redesign?)

League (1919) lacked universal membership, had weak enforcement, and the US never joined. WWII proved the need for a stronger body with a Security Council able to act on threats, a General Assembly for universal voice, and a permanent Secretariat. The UN is not a world government — it is a member-state organization bound by the Charter.

Recognition vs membership: The UN does not “recognize” new states like a government does. It admits members or accepts credentials of representatives. (UN membership FAQ)

Elections, votes, money — how the machine works

सदस्यता = SC सिफारिश + GA दो-तिहाई · SG = SC नाम + GA नियुक्ति · UNSC गैर-स्थायी = हर साल GA चुनाव
Charter + GA Rules of Procedure. Not every post is “elected by all countries equally” — regional groups matter.

1. Admitting a new member state

  1. State sends application to the Secretary-General with formal acceptance of Charter obligations (GA Rule 134).
  2. SG circulates application to GA members / SC.
  3. Security Council considers whether the state is peace-loving and able/willing to meet obligations; usually via a committee of the whole (35+ days before GA session per SC rules).
  4. SC recommendation needs 9 affirmative votes among 15 members; no P5 veto against the applicant.
  5. General Assembly decides by two-thirds majority of members present and voting (Rule 136).
  6. Membership effective the date GA adopts the admission resolution.

2. Secretary-General — selection & role

  • Art. 97: Appointed by GA on recommendation of SC; chief administrative officer.
  • Process: Member states may nominate candidates → SC private meetings & straw polls (modern practice) → SC resolution recommending one name → GA private meeting, secret ballot (since 1946, res. 11(I)).
  • Term: Five years, renewable (custom). Regional rotation is strong informal norm (e.g. Western Europe, Africa, Asia-Pacific, Latin America).
  • Art. 99: May bring to SC attention any matter threatening international peace.
  • Art. 100: Staff independent — no instructions from any government.
  • GA resolutions 73/341, 79/327 — more transparency in selection; next appointment cycle follows joint PGA–SC letters (e.g. 2026 process).
  • Current SG: António Guterres (Portugal); appointed 2016, re-appointed 2021.

3. President of the General Assembly (PGA)

  • Elected for one session (about a year, Sept–Sept rhythm) in personal capacity, represents whole membership.
  • Elected at least three months before session opens (often mid-June) — GA Rule 30.
  • Rotates among five regional groups (equitable geographical rotation — GA res. 33/138 pattern).
  • Regional group usually agrees on one candidate (“clean slate”) → election by acclamation or simple majority.
  • Also elects 21 Vice-Presidents and coordinates with chairs of six Main Committees.
  • Convention: Permanent SC members do not field candidates for PGA.
  • Example (CA): PGA for 81st session (2026–27) from Asia-Pacific — elected June 2026.

5. ICJ judges — parallel election

  • 15 judges, 9-year terms; one-third elected every 3 years (triennial election, autumn NY).
  • GA and SC vote separately but simultaneously — must win absolute majority in both (e.g. 97/193 in GA, 8/15 in SC).
  • Nomination route includes national groups in Permanent Court of Arbitration lists.
  • No two judges of same nationality; informal regional balance.
  • Court elects its President & Vice-President for 3 years; seat: Peace Palace, The Hague.
  • India: Dalveer Bhandari re-elected 2017 after GA–SC deadlock with UK candidate (classic IR case study).

6. ECOSOC & Human Rights Council

  • ECOSOC: 54 members, elected by GA for 3-year terms; regional distribution; coordinates agencies & commissions.

8. Budget & assessments

  • Regular budget approved by GA (Fifth Committee prepares; Committee on Contributions sets scales).
  • Peacekeeping — separate scale; SC authorizes missions; GA approves resources.
  • Assessed contributions based on capacity to pay; P5 pay largest shares on regular budget (with ceilings/discounts — know concept).
  • Art. 17: expenses borne by members as apportioned by GA.

9. How a GA session runs (sketch)

  • Main session opens annually (September); General Debate — heads of state/government speak.
  • Work split into six Main Committees + plenary; PGA chairs plenary; bureau of VPs.

B. Political blocs at the UN (not Charter organs)

  • G77 + China — developing countries' coalition in UN economic negotiations (not 77 members today).
  • NAM — Non-Aligned Movement; Cold War legacy; consultative, not a UN body.
  • G4 — India, Brazil, Germany, Japan — mutual support for UNSC permanent seats.
  • P5 — permanent UNSC members with veto (not a "group" in Charter, but exam shorthand).
  • EU — coordinates voting at UN on many issues (27 states).

Linked to "UN" in news — but different legal animal

COP = संधि की बैठक · IAEA = परमाणु · WTO = व्यापार · ICC = अपराध अदालत

Multilateral Environmental Agreements (MEAs)

UNFCCC, CBD, CITES, Montreal Protocol, Ramsar…

States are Parties. Supreme body often called COP (Conference of the Parties). Secretariats may be hosted in UN system (e.g. climate secretariat linked to UNEP history) but the treaty is boss. Full ladder → UN Environment Architecture.

IAEA

International Atomic Energy Agency — Vienna

Autonomous; reports to UNGA & UNSC. Safeguards, nuclear verification. Not one of 15 specialized agencies in the classic list sense — "related organization".

WTO

World Trade Organization — Geneva

Trade rules; successor to GATT 1947. Cooperates with UN system but not a UN specialized agency. Details → IO trade tab.

ICC

International Criminal Court — The Hague

Rome Statute 1998 — tries individuals for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes. Not a UN organ (often confused with ICJ).

IPCC

Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change

Scientific assessments for policymakers; supports UNFCCC negotiations. Does not set law or run COP.

Peacekeeping — how a mission is born

  1. UNSC resolution defines mandate (observe, protect civilians, support peace agreements, etc.).
  2. Member states volunteer military, police, experts — UN has no standing army.
  3. Department of Peace Operations (field missions) & political missions under Secretariat management.
  4. Financed by peacekeeping budget (GA-approved; separate assessment scale from regular budget).
  5. Mission ends when SC ends mandate or situation stabilizes.

Three principles (classic doctrine): consent of parties; impartiality; minimum use of force except in self-defence and defence of the mandate.

When peace fails — Chapter VII toolkit

  • Art. 41: measures not involving armed force (sanctions, embargoes, breaking relations).
  • Art. 42: armed action by member states or UN as SC decides.
  • Sanctions committees under SC monitor compliance (country-specific lists — track CA).
  • Uniting for Peace (1950): if SC deadlocked, GA can recommend collective action on peace & security (emergency special sessions).

Trusteeship Council — what it did

  • Supervised trust territories (former mandates/colonies) until self-government or independence.
  • Last territory: Palau (1994); Council suspended operations but remains a sixth principal organ in the Charter.

Secretariat — day-to-day UN

  • International civil service under SG; prepares studies, runs field offices, implements GA/SC decisions.
  • Art. 100: staff must not seek/receive instructions from any government.
  • SG selection, oath, five-year term → see How it runs.

1. How many principal organs does the UN have under the Charter?
a) Six
b) Seven
c) Fifteen
d) Same as specialized agencies
a) GA, SC, ECOSOC, Trusteeship, ICJ, Secretariat. WHO is an agency, not an organ.
2. Which principal organ is inactive but still in the Charter?
a) ECOSOC
b) Trusteeship Council
c) ICJ
d) UNGA
b) No trust territories remain.
3. India sits in which UN regional group for electoral purposes?
a) Asia-Pacific Group
b) WEOG
c) African Group
d) GRULAC
a) APG.
4. UNEP headquarters is in:
a) Geneva
b) New York
c) Nairobi
d) Paris
c) Programme born from Stockholm 1972 conference.
5. COP (Conference of the Parties) usually refers to:
a) UN General Assembly annual session
b) Supreme body of a multilateral treaty such as UNFCCC
c) UNSC meeting
d) WTO ministerial
b) Treaty parties, not UN organ.
6. ICJ is located in:
a) New York
b) The Hague
c) Geneva
d) Vienna
b) ICC is also Hague but different court (individuals).
7. How many UN specialized agencies are in the standard UN list?
a) 6
b) 15
c) 193
d) 77
b) See Agencies tab table.
8. UN Charter entered into force on:
a) 26 June 1945
b) 24 October 1945
c) 1 January 1942
d) 15 October 1945
b) UN Day. Charter signed 26 June; Poland signed 15 Oct.
9. Secretary-General is appointed under:
a) GA alone
b) GA on recommendation of Security Council
c) ICJ nomination
d) ECOSOC vote
b) Art. 97.
10. Peacekeeping forces are authorized by:
a) UNGA alone always
b) UNSC (primary)
c) ICJ
d) WHO
b) SC mandates; GA role in some financing/policy debates.
11. How many official UN languages under the Charter?
a) 4
b) 6
c) 5
d) 2
b) Art. 111 — Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish.
12. New UN membership requires GA vote of:
a) Simple majority
b) Two-thirds of members present and voting
c) Unanimity
d) SC only
b) After SC recommendation (Art. 4, GA Rule 136).
13. ICJ judges serve for:
a) 5 years
b) 9 years
c) Life
d) 6 years
b) One-third elected every 3 years.
