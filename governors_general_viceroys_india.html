Each rename tracks a real shift of power: Company presidencies → one Bengal boss → one India legislature → Crown’s royal agent → ceremonial Dominion head, then Republic.

⚜️ Governors-General of Bengal (1773–1833)
📜 Regulating Act, 1773 Pitt’s India Act, 1784 Charter Act, 1813

Before: three separate Company “Governors” (Bengal, Bombay, Madras). Robert Clive was Governor of Bengal, not of India. After Plassey/Buxar the Company was a territorial power, but London had almost no handle on it.

Why the new title? Bengal was the richest presidency. Parliament made its Governor the Governor-General of Bengal so Bombay & Madras answered to one man — first central authority.
What did not change yet? He was still of Bengal, not of India. Madras/Bombay kept their own armies and a lot of independence in practice.
1. Warren Hastings
1773 – 1785
Regulating Act 1773Pitt’s India Act 1784
  • First Governor-General of Bengal (post created by Regulating Act, 1773); council of 4 — often outvoted (Francis, Clavering, Monson vs Barwell)
  • Ended Dual Government in Bengal (1772, as Governor before the new title) — Company took diwani + nizamat
  • Rohilla War (1774); First Anglo-Maratha War & Treaty of Salbai (1782); Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–84)
  • Calcutta Madrasa (1781); Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784, with William Jones)
  • Conflict with Supreme Court (1774) — Raja Nand Kumar hanged for forgery, 1775 (exam favourite)
+ more facts
  • Policy of Ring Fence: buffer allies to shield Company territory
  • First translation push: Bhagavad Gita into English (Charles Wilkins, 1785)
  • Impeached in Britain by Burke/Sheridan; acquitted after ~7 years
  • Pitt’s India Act, 1784 passed during his term — Board of Control over politics

2. Sir John Macpherson
1785 – 1786
Acting/caretaker after Hastings; ~20 months. Avoided wars to patch Company finances. Nicknamed among the most jobbery-prone GGs — no landmark Act or war. Stop-gap before Cornwallis arrived with a reform mandate.

🏛️ Governors-General of India (1833–1858)
📜 Charter Act, 1833 Charter Act, 1853

By the 1830s the Company ruled far beyond Bengal. Calling the boss “of Bengal” was a lie on the map. Charter Act 1833 killed the remaining trade monopoly, turned the Company into a pure administrative trustee of the Crown, and renamed the post Governor-General of India.

Legal punch: only the GG-in-Council could now make laws for all British India. Bombay & Madras lost independent law-making (until 1861 restored a slice).
First holder: William Bentinck (already in office as GG of Bengal) — same man, new all-India title from 1833.

👑 Viceroys of India (1858–1947)
📜 Government of India Act, 1858 Councils 1861 1909 1919 1935

1857 showed a trading company could not hold an empire. Parliament abolished the Company. The GG stayed as head of the Indian government, but also became Viceroy — “in place of the king” — the Crown’s personal representative, named by the British government, not Company directors.

Two hats, one person: Governor-General = executive head of British India. Viceroy = royal face, especially vs princely states (paramountcy).
London end: Court of Directors gone; Secretary of State for India + India Council (15) run policy from Britain.

🕊️ Free India's Governor-General (1947–1950)
📜 Indian Independence Act, 1947

The Viceroy title dies with British India. Independent India is a Dominion: sovereign Cabinet, but the British Monarch is still ceremonial head of state, represented by a Governor-General chosen on Indian advice. That lasts until the Constitution — 26 January 1950 — when the office is abolished for the President.

Mountbatten: last Viceroy, then first GG of the Dominion (till June 1948).
Rajaji: only Indian GG, last holder of the post.

📜 Landmark Acts — tap to open

Regulating → Pitt’s → Charters → Crown takeover → Councils → 1919 → 1935 → Independence. Each card: why / key points / impact.

Regulating Act 1773
Why Company after Plassey/Buxar was a state without a state. Bengal famine 1770 + corruption in London forced Parliament to “regulate”.
Key points Governor of Bengal → GG of Bengal + 4-member Council. Bombay & Madras under Bengal. Supreme Court at Calcutta, 1774. Directors must report to the British government.
Impact First written Constitution for Company India. Hastings is first GG. Dual-control seed. SC vs Council clashes (Nand Kumar).
Trap: Clive was Governor of Bengal, not GG. Title “GG of India” is 1833, not 1773.
Pitt’s India Act 1784
Why 1773 left the Company still too independent; Fox’s India Bill had failed. Pitt needed a workable dual system.
Key points Board of Control (6 privy councillors) for political/military affairs. Court of Directors keep commerce & patronage. GG Council cut to 3. Indian possessions called “British possessions”.
Impact Company is a Crown subordinate in politics. Dual government till 1858. Cornwallis arrives with this machine.
Trap: Board of Control ≠ Secretary of State (that is 1858).
Charter Act 1793
Why 20-year Company charter was up. Trade lobby vs Company monopoly.
Key points Monopoly extended 20 years. Board of Control salaries paid from Indian revenues. GG can override his Council. Seniority for promotions.
Impact Status quo: Company still trader + ruler. Cornwallis Code / Permanent Settlement sit in the same year.
Charter Act 1813
Why Napoleon wars + British private merchants want India. Evangelicals want missions.
Key points Company trade monopoly ends except tea & China. ₹1 lakh/year for education. Christian missionaries allowed. Crown’s sovereignty over Company territories asserted.
Impact Free-trade breach. Education grant = later Macaulay/Woods. Missions legally in. Minto I / Hastings era.
Trap: Full trade monopoly death is 1833, not 1813 (tea & China stayed).
Charter Act 1833
Why Company as a merchant in an industrial Britain looked absurd. Need one legislature for a subcontinent.
Key points Company loses remaining commercial monopoly — purely administrative trustee. GG of Bengal → GG of India. Bombay/Madras lose law-making. 4th member = Law Member (Macaulay). Indian Law Commission. Sec. 87: no disability by religion, place of birth, colour (on paper).
Impact Bentinck first GG of India. Centralised legislation. First all-India statutory title.
Trap: Indians were not actually thrown into ICS in 1833 — open competition is 1853/55.
Charter Act 1853
Why Last Company charter. Macaulay’s ICS idea + need for a real legislature.
Key points No 20-year renewal — Company to serve “until Parliament provides otherwise”. Open competition for ICS (first exam 1855). Separate Legislative Council (6 members). Law Member becomes a full Council member. Local representation (one member each from Madras, Bombay, Bengal, NWFP).
Impact Haileybury patronage dies. Dalhousie’s years. Prelude to 1858 — Company already on notice.
Government of India Act 1858
Why 1857. A joint-stock company cannot hold an empire. Queen’s Proclamation, 1 Nov 1858.
Key points Company abolished. Power to the Crown. Secretary of State for India + 15-member India Council. GG also Viceroy. Doctrine of Lapse dumped in practice; princes promised their izzat.
Impact Canning first Viceroy. Dual Co. government (Board + Directors) gone. Paramountcy now royal.
Trap: “Viceroy” was extra hat; legal executive title stayed Governor-General.
Indian Councils Act 1861
Why 1857 showed Indians must be heard, at least as nominees. Portfolio government needed.
Key points Indians nominated to Viceroy’s Legislative Council. Portfolio / Cabinet-style executive. Madras & Bombay get legislative power back. Viceroy can issue ordinances.
Impact Seed of representative institutions — still nomination, not election. Canning.
Indian Councils Act 1892
Why INC (from 1885) asking for elections and budget voice. Lansdowne years.
Key points Councils enlarged. Indirect election via “recommendation”. Members can discuss budget and ask questions (not supplementary; cannot vote the budget down).
Impact First sniff of election. Still official majority. Bridge to 1909.
Indian Councils Act / Morley–Minto 1909
Why Swadeshi after 1905; Muslim elite want a separate political share; British “divide and rule” fit.
Key points Separate electorates for Muslims. Indians on Executive Councils (S.P. Sinha at Centre). Councils bigger; still unelected official core. Non-official majority in some provincial councils, not at Centre.
Impact Communal electorate becomes the default path (Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians later). Minto II.
Trap: Dyarchy is 1919, not 1909. First Indian cabinet minister at Centre ≠ responsible government.
Government of India Act / Montagu–Chelmsford 1919
Why WWI bargains + Montagu Declaration 1917: gradual responsible government.
Key points Dyarchy in provinces — reserved (police, finance, land) vs transferred (education, health) subjects. Bicameral Centre (Council of State + Assembly). Direct elections. Separate electorates extended. Public Service Commission promised.
Impact First taste of ministers answering a legislature — only on “soft” subjects. Chelmsford. Dyarchy widely called a failure. Jallianwala / Rowlatt in the same year poison the reforms.
Government of India Act 1935
Why Simon Commission, RTCs, White Paper. Longest British statute on India.
Key points All-India Federation (British provinces + opt-in princes) — never came into being. Provincial autonomy; dyarchy abolished in provinces, introduced at Centre (never worked). Bicameralism in 6 provinces. Federal Court (1937). RBI already 1934 but in this federal design. Separate electorates expanded (incl. depressed classes after Poona Pact as reserved seats). 10% adult franchise roughly.
Impact 1937 elections; Congress ministries. Huge borrow-list for the 1950 Constitution (federation, GOI residual, federal court → SC). Willingdon / Linlithgow.
Trap: Federation in the Act ≠ federation in practice. Princely states did not accede to it.
Indian Independence Act 1947
Why Cabinet Mission stalled; League’s Pakistan demand; Attlee’s Feb 1947 deadline; Mountbatten Plan 3 June.
Key points Two Dominions, 15 Aug 1947. British paramountcy over princes lapses. Constituent Assemblies become sovereign legislatures. Office of Viceroy ends; each Dominion gets a GG. Secretary of State for India abolished.
Impact Partition, Radcliffe, massive migration. Mountbatten then Rajaji as GGs until 26 Jan 1950 Republic.
⭐ The Most Exam-Important Milestones
1773
Regulating Act
Creates the post of Governor-General of Bengal; Warren Hastings first to hold it.
1793
Permanent Settlement
Cornwallis fixes Bengal's land revenue system permanently with zamindars.
1829
Abolition of Sati
Bentinck bans Sati with Raja Ram Mohan Roy's support.
1833
Charter Act
Title changes to Governor-General of India; Bentinck becomes the first holder.
1848–56
Doctrine of Lapse
Dalhousie annexes princely states without natural heirs; also starts railways & telegraph.
1858
GoI Act 1858
Crown takes direct control from the Company; Canning becomes the first Viceroy.
1885
INC Founded
Indian National Congress formed during Dufferin's viceroyalty.
1905
Partition of Bengal
Curzon's most controversial act — undone by Hardinge II in 1911.
1919
Jallianwala Bagh
Rowlatt Act & the massacre under Chelmsford; GoI Act 1919 also passed.
1930–32
Civil Disobedience
Dandi March, Round Table Conferences & Poona Pact span Irwin and Willingdon.
1942
Quit India Movement
Launched under Linlithgow after the failed Cripps Mission.
1946
Cabinet Mission
Wavell's key initiative for transfer of power talks.
1947
Independence & Partition
Mountbatten executes the June 3rd Plan — India freed and partitioned.
1950
Office Abolished
Rajagopalachari is the last Governor-General; Republic of India begins, 26 Jan 1950.
🔄 Why the Title Changed — 4 Turning Points
① 1773
Regulating Act
Independent Governor of Bengal (Clive’s world) → GG of Bengal. One boss over Bombay & Madras. Still not “of India”.
② 1833
Charter Act
GG of Bengal → GG of India. Company stops trading. One all-India legislature. Bentinck first holder.
③ 1858
GoI Act
Same man is GG and Viceroy. Company dead; Crown’s face vs princes. Canning first Viceroy.
④ 1947–50
IIA + Republic
Viceroy dies. Dominion GG is ceremonial (Mountbatten, then Rajaji). 26 Jan 1950 → President.
🎴 Quick Recall Flashcards (tap a card to flip)
Who was the FIRST Governor-General of Bengal?
Warren Hastings (1773) — post created by the Regulating Act, 1773.
Who was the FIRST Governor-General of India?
Lord William Bentinck (1833) — post renamed by the Charter Act, 1833.
Who was the FIRST Viceroy of India?
Lord Canning (1858) — same person as the last Governor-General.
Who was the LAST Viceroy of India?
Lord Mountbatten (1947) — oversaw Independence & Partition.
Who partitioned Bengal in 1905?
Lord Curzon — later annulled by Lord Hardinge II in 1911.
Under whom was the INC founded?
Lord Dufferin (1885).
Who was assassinated in office, in the Andamans?
Lord Mayo (1872) — only Viceroy assassinated in India.
Who was the ONLY Indian Governor-General?
C. Rajagopalachari (1948–50) — office abolished in 1950.
Who actually enacted the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856?
Lord Canning — it was only drafted under Dalhousie, not passed by him.
Which Act changed "Governor of Bengal" to "Governor-General of Bengal"?
The Regulating Act, 1773.
Which Act changed the title to "Governor-General of India"?
The Charter Act, 1833 — first held by Lord William Bentinck.
Which Act created the title "Viceroy"?
The Government of India Act, 1858, after the Revolt of 1857.
Dalhousie = social reformer?
No. Annexation + rails/telegraph/PWD. Sati = Bentinck. Widow remarriage Act = Canning.
Pitt’s India Act, 1784 — big idea?
Dual government: Board of Control (politics) vs Court of Directors (commerce).
When did Company lose ALL trade monopoly?
Charter Act 1833. 1813 had already opened India except tea & China.
Open competition for ICS — which Act?
Charter Act, 1853 (first exam 1855). Not 1833.
Separate electorates begin?
Indian Councils Act / Morley–Minto, 1909 — Muslims.
Dyarchy in provinces?
GoI Act 1919. 1935 kills provincial dyarchy, tries (and fails) dyarchy at the Centre.
Did the 1935 All-India Federation start?
No. Princes never joined. Provincial autonomy did (1937 ministries).
